Check the AI

A university research project, 5–10 minutes

Is the AI's answer good enough?

We are testing an AI helper built into smart glasses. It sees what you see and does small jobs for you, like searching the web or adding something to your calendar. You check whether it typed in the right thing. No knowledge about AI is needed.

How it works

  1. Read what is happening. For example: someone is in a bookshop, looking at a new novel.
  2. Compare two answers: what a person wrote, and what the AI wrote.
  3. Press one button. Is the AI's answer just as useful? Yes, Partly or No.

Good to know

  • There is no right or wrong for you. We want your first impression.
  • The person's answer is just one good answer. Other words for the same thing count as Yes.
  • Some questions are hard on purpose. Pick what feels closest and move on.
  • About 10 seconds per question. Answer as many as you like: 20 already help a lot.
  • Your answers are sent automatically. No sign-up and no personal details.

What is collected, and how it is used

For each question: your choice (Correct, Partial or Incorrect), an optional note and the time of the answer, together with your nickname and a random code that keeps people with the same nickname apart. Nothing else is collected: no account, no email address, no cookies.

Your progress is also kept in this browser so you can continue later. Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at any time. The answers are used only to measure how well an AI assistant fills in tool calls in a research paper; only aggregated results are published.

How to answer

Is the AI's answer just as useful for the person as the answer a person wrote?

  • Yes, just as usefulIt would help just as well. Other words for the same thing are fine.
  • PartlySame subject, but it asks for something a bit different or narrower, or one part is wrong.
  • NoSomething else, or it would not help.

Only judge what the AI wrote, not whether its job was a good idea. “Found automatically” means the glasses fill it in themselves, for example your current address from GPS. There is no right or wrong for you: go with your first impression.
